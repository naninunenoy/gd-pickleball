# Wall rally rules

Choose wall practice or a singles/doubles volley exchange, controlled on the same phone. Play upright on a phone and try to beat your longest rally. All in-game text stays English.

## Game selection

The left header selector switches **WALL RALLY / VOLLEY RALLY** before play, after a miss, or while paused. Changing game restarts the rally and cancels pending input. Your game choice, separate wall/volley player counts and separate best records are saved. Both selectors disappear during play, leaving the whole screen available for shots.

## Singles and doubles volley rally

- Use the right selector to choose SINGLES (1v1) or DOUBLES (2v2) before play, after a miss, or while paused. Both use the same phone and full-screen input. Your racket appears in the foreground; the center marker indicates when the ball is in reach.
- Tap anywhere to start or return when you lift your finger. A still tap stays in the same lateral lane. There is no hold power; gesture duration never adds speed.
- A short swipe plays a soft dink into the opposing kitchen; a longer swipe plays a deep stroke. Left/right chooses the side; longer up/down swipes launch upward/downward. Diagonals combine side and stroke. The preview arrow is blue for a dink and green for a deep stroke; small finger movement is ignored.
- Ball speed is 65% of normal wall-practice speed. Initial singles flights take roughly 0.86–0.93 seconds, with gentle acceleration limited to a 90% duration floor. The prepared contact is higher so downward strokes can clear the net, and the opponent moves to meet each course.
- A released tap or swipe can wait for the next incoming ball, even when entered during your outgoing flight. It is consumed once when the ball enters racket reach. Pressing never hits automatically.
- A press just before the ball passes gives up to 0.2 seconds to release. The ball stays visible at the endpoint during this grace; holding longer still misses.
- A dink bounces in the kitchen before the opponent picks it up and dinks back. Your receiver moves into the kitchen for the bounced ball. An early tap waits until after the bounce; a tap on the low ball automatically plays another dink. A long swipe can send it deep instead.
- A landing ring marks the dink's bounce. The camera follows forward and looks lower so the pickup stays visible. A second bounce ends the rally. A fresh tap retries.
- Unbounced volleys require the player's feet outside the kitchen, including its boundary line. Bounced balls may be struck inside it. After a hit, recover toward the line.
- Opponents return automatically. Your successful returns count toward the separate volley record; serving and ground bounces do not count.
- In doubles, P1 covers the left half and P2 the right. The course selects the far-side CPU receiver. Opponents return crosscourt, and the resulting near-side course selects P1 or P2; central balls are yielded to the partner. Selection follows the course rather than a hit counter.
- After a hit, return toward your own home lane. Only the receiving athlete approaches the next ball; the partner stays in their own half. The camera stays centered and shows both lanes while the header names P1/P2 TO HIT. Receiver changes and retry taps do not shift the view sideways. No player-switch button is needed.
- Tap after a miss to restart, or use the header selector to return to wall practice. Singles/doubles and wall player count are remembered independently; singles and doubles share the volley personal best.

## Wall practice taps and holds

- The entire screen accepts input, including the score, instructions and build footer. There are no shot or player-switch buttons during a rally.
- A short tap (under 0.2 seconds) serves or returns at normal speed when you lift your finger. You do not need to hold for normal play.
- Let the wall's return bounce once. A slightly early normal tap waits up to 0.35 seconds for the ball to reach the racket.
- You can tap after the highest point while the ball is falling and still within arm/racket reach. Normal taps and strong shots share this descending reach; you do not need power to hit a falling ball.
- Hold for at least 0.2 seconds for optional power, then release when the ball is near your racket after one bounce. A longer hold gives a faster shot, capped at 0.75 seconds and 1.6 times normal speed. Its wall return keeps that speed.
- Swipe before lifting your finger to aim a normal or strong shot. Left/right sends the ball left/right; up/down sends it higher/lower against the wall. Diagonal swipes combine both. An arrow follows your swipe. Small finger movements are ignored, and a still tap sends the ball straight toward the wall.
- You can start holding while the ball is outside racket range. Holding never hits automatically; release judges the strong shot. One gesture produces either a normal shot or a strong shot, never both.
- Power allows extra fore/aft racket reach while the ball rises. During descent, ordinary taps and strong shots have the same extended reach. An early strong release keeps both its direction and speed until this ball reaches the racket. The screen shows "Waiting for the ball." A release after a missed second bounce cannot restart the rally; a fresh press is required.
- Your player anticipates the highest point after the first bounce, moves there, and waits with the right-hand racket beside the rebound.
- Every successful return counts once. Serving does not count. A short ring and spark burst marks a successful hit.
- A second ground bounce ends the rally. Tap to retry; your personal best stays saved.

Swiping is optional; ordinary taps still work. No double-tapping or shot selection is required. A pause, app switch or canceled touch cancels a hold and queued input. The rally gradually speeds up with its length, and wall returns follow the shot's lateral direction within the lane. Shots without a swipe travel straight out and back; return count never changes their direction.

## One or two players

Use the **1 PLAYER / 2 PLAYERS** selector before serving, after a second bounce, or while paused. It disappears during play so the whole screen continues to accept shots. Changing mode starts a fresh rally, cancels held/queued shots, and keeps your shared best. Your chosen mode is remembered next time.

In two-player practice, P1 wears cream and P2 wears blue. Their labels identify them; only the next receiver has a glowing foot ring. P1 owns the left side and P2 owns the right. The wall return's course determines the next receiver, even when the previous hitter is currently closer. Balls near the middle are yielded to the partner. After every strike, both athletes recover toward their own home positions. They do not run toward a predicted strike point before the ball hits the wall. After the wall rebound, only the selected receiver approaches the highest point after the ground bounce, with limited overlap near the middle. That athlete owns the next tap, hold or swipe and remains selected until the next successful hit. The other athlete remains at their own home position and leaves the partner's side clear. A player can receive consecutive balls: course determines the receiver, not a fixed turn order. Holds may start before the ball is in range. The previous hitter completes their own swing even after the receiver changes.

## Screen and pause

The game uses a portrait court view. Wider screens retain the same tall play area. All on-screen information lets taps through. Leaving the app or tab pauses an active rally. Tap anywhere to resume without hitting.

Desktop: click or Space / Enter uses the same short-tap/hold gesture. Drag before releasing the mouse to aim; keyboard-only shots travel straight. P / Escape pauses. R resets the rally. Personal best survives retries and can be reset by clearing local site data.
